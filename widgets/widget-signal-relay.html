    How to play

    Two trains run the same line, one after the other. Train A leaves with and train B with . Each car holds one sign.

    Signals change the sign on every car that passes. Press a signal to choose what it does: leave the sign alone, set a sign, or swap a pair of signs. Both trains pass the same signals.

    The checks above the line say what each train must be carrying at each station. Dispatch to run both trains and score them.

    The recorder keeps what each car carried at every point. Shaded columns are where A and B carried the same sign. Press a column heading to see the trains there.

    The telegraph can restore an earlier sign. Press a signal and pick a point under Telegraph: that signal gives every car back the sign it carried there. There is one link, so only one signal can restore.

    The idea behind the game

    Carrying information down the line

    Each car carries a single sign, and every signal it passes can rewrite it. A recurrent model reads a sequence the same way: it keeps one fixed-size state and updates it at every step with the same rule. Whatever an early step needs to tell a later one has to survive every update in between.

    Some updates keep information and some destroy it. Once two trains carry the same sign, nothing further down the line can tell them apart from the car alone.

    A railway has other ways to keep track: the route a train is on, or a telegraph that reads back what happened earlier on the line. Attention gives a model this kind of direct access to earlier inputs, choosing what to read by where it was or by what it contains. The game uses exact matches; attention blends many earlier inputs, weighted by how well each one matches.
