A working reference for course developers

Interaction methods for course design

What does the learner do, and what comes back? Choose the action for the unit's question, then decide its job: the decision in a gamified opener, or the fix a learner presses in an unassessed summary.

38 actions in 7 families. In a summary, 11 keep the learner's action and 20 more work as a demonstration of the method. The sketches are authored schematics with no data encoded. The visual methods catalog covers what the marks mean; this one covers what the learner does with them.

Use this catalog
  1. Start from the unit's question. Pick an action whose feedback makes the idea visible.
  2. Every action returns visible feedback at once. Duration can be the action when it changes a meaningful state, as holding the shoot button sets the power in the Pudding's crokinole essay; holding only to wait shows nothing.
  3. Read both forms on each card. In an opener the action is the decision the game scores. A summary form either keeps the learner's action, used to supply or mark the problem, or turns it into a demonstration in which the method acts and the learner presses the fix.
  4. Give every action a keyboard route that performs the task itself, not only reads back its result, and give every sound a visual and text route that carries the same evidence. No decision depends on a picture or a sound alone.
  5. Pairings on each card are examples, not a menu. Any action can meet any visual method; the test is whether the learner can see why their action mattered. Presentation patterns such as disclosure and linked highlighting work with any action; the visual catalog lists them.

01 · Commit, then see · Openers

Call the run

Choose before the system acts, then watch it act.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Commits to one choice, such as which case will fail, before anything runs.
What comes back
The run plays out on every case, so the call is settled on screen.
As an opener
Pick which of three photos the detector will miss, then run it. The next round brings a set whose answer differs.
As a summary
Weak fit. A call with a right answer is a check, and summary pages carry none.
Watch for
A single guess teaches little. Make the answer change between rounds, and keep separate prediction questions on the course page, outside the game.
Keyboard and text route
Choices are named buttons; the outcome of the run is announced in one short status line.
Pairs with, for example
  • Matched small multiples: Three photos in matched frames; after the call, each frame shows the detector's boxes, so the miss is found by comparing like with like.

Our games: Beach Beeps (digs or walks on at each spot, judged on the odds)

Looser inspiration: The Pour-igin of Species

02 · Commit, then see · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Place a bet

Spread a stake over the outcomes and let the outcome pay out.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Spreads ten chips across the possible outcomes.
What comes back
The outcome lands; the chips on it pay and the rest are lost.
As an opener
Bet on the next word of a sentence and let the real text pay out. The verdict has two halves: whether the spread was right on the odds, and whether this round bore it out.
As a summary
The model places the chips. Open on an unsmoothed model with no chips on unseen words, then press the fix and watch smoothing move a few onto them.
Watch for
One round is luck. Judge the spread against fixed odds, not against the round that happened.
Keyboard and text route
Each outcome has add and remove buttons with its chip count beside the stack.
Pairs with, for example
  • Paired count strips: The learner's chips and the counts the text actually produced, as paired bars for each candidate word.
  • Counted unit tiles: Each chip is one tile, so a moved chip is a visible unit and the total of ten never hides.

Direct example: How rigid is the middle class in the US, really?

03 · Commit, then see · Openers

Seal the rule

Write a rule, seal it, then open the cases it has to handle.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Writes or builds a rule before seeing the cases it will face.
What comes back
The unseen cases open one at a time, and each passes or fails under the sealed rule.
As an opener
Write where a word should split; 20 new words then test the rule. The win is a rule that holds on words you never saw.
As a summary
Weak fit. Sealing only matters when the learner's rule is scored.
Watch for
The hidden cases must come from the same population as the examples shown, or a reasonable rule gets punished.
Keyboard and text route
The rule is built from labelled choices or typed text; results come as a list as well as a picture.
Pairs with, for example
  • Example contact sheet: The unseen cases open as a contact sheet, each tile marked pass or fail under the sealed rule.

04 · Commit, then see · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Rate, then see the crowd

Give your own rating, then see everyone else's.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Rates a case on a short scale.
What comes back
Other people's ratings appear as a distribution, with the learner's own marked and the model's rating beside it.
As an opener
Rate how alike two images look, then see how people in a reference dataset and the model rated the same pair. The rating stays on the page; nothing is collected.
As a summary
Show people's ratings from a reference dataset beside the method's. Press the fix and see how the method's ratings change against people's.
Watch for
The crowd can come from a published reference dataset, with no new collection. Say who rated, beside the chart.
Keyboard and text route
The scale is a set of radio buttons; the crowd chart has a sentence giving the share who chose the learner's rating.
Pairs with, for example
  • Paired count strips: For each image pair, the learner's rating, the crowd's and the model's as dots on one scale.
  • Observed against reference: The learner's rating placed on the distribution of everyone's ratings, with that reference's source one click away.

Direct example: How Music Is Remembered: the music challenge

05 · Commit, then see · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Set a range

Commit to a range before the outcome lands, paying for every bit of width.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Sets the low and high ends of a range under a stated cost for width.
What comes back
Outcomes land inside or outside the range, and the cost of its width is charged.
As an opener
Set tomorrow's temperature range for a picnic planner. Each degree of width costs a point, and each miss costs ten.
As a summary
Show the old method's ranges missing more often than they claim. Press the fix and see the new ranges' coverage on the same outcomes.
Watch for
Judge a range against its stated odds over many outcomes. For a single round, the verdict has two halves: whether the range was right on the odds, and whether this outcome bore it out.
Keyboard and text route
Each end moves with the arrow keys; the width, the cost and the outcomes inside and outside are given as numbers.
Pairs with, for example
  • Outcomes with an interval band: Each committed range drawn as a band over the outcomes that followed, with the misses counted.
  • Paired count strips: For each round, the range width and whether the outcome landed inside, as paired marks.

Our games: Tipping Point (places the support so that 9 drops in 10 hold, a range with one end)

06 · Make · Openers and summaries

Build from parts

Assemble a system from pieces and watch it run.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Adds, removes or arranges pieces.
What comes back
The assembled system runs at once on the same input.
As an opener
Build the smallest stack of pieces that still sorts the bins. Fewest pieces wins.
As a summary
Each slide adds one block when the learner presses for it. The block clears the failure the last one left, on the same example.
Watch for
Too many kinds of piece turn play into bookkeeping. Offer a few, at the level of the concept.
Keyboard and text route
Pieces are added from a list with buttons, and the stack is read out in order.
Pairs with, for example
  • Same-scene layers: Each added block lays its contribution over the same input, so the learner sees what that block changed.

Our games: Recycling Day (chooses the pieces; training sets the weights) · Lookout Tower (stacks floors and sets how often the lookouts halve) · Backup Power (wires lamps to generators)

Direct example: Wonky

07 · Make · Openers and summaries

Draw freehand

Sketch the answer, then lay the true one over it.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Draws a boundary, an edge, a curve or a route.
What comes back
The method's version or the true one is drawn over the sketch, and the gap between them is shaded.
As an opener
Draw the outline of the object in a photo; the filter's outline then appears over yours.
As a summary
Show the old method's output and let the learner circle what it got wrong. Press the fix and the circled area shrinks.
Watch for
Freehand accuracy depends on the device. Score the shape of the answer, not pixel precision, or let the learner place a few control points instead of a free line.
Keyboard and text route
A version with draggable points moved by arrow keys, and the overlap described in words.
Pairs with, for example
  • Same-scene layers: The learner's outline and the method's as two layers over one photo, with the gap shaded.

Our games: Rover Route (places up to four bends; the route refits to the markers)

08 · Make · Openers and summaries

Type your own

The learner supplies the input, so the example is theirs.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Types a word, a sentence or a search.
What comes back
The system responds to that exact input.
As an opener
Write a sentence the robot gets wrong. Shorter wins.
As a summary
Ask for one sentence at the top, then carry it through every method on the page. Offer a default so the page works without typing.
Watch for
Open text can produce cases the page was not built for. Limit the vocabulary, or fall back to the default with a note.
Keyboard and text route
A labelled text field; the output appears as text.
Pairs with, for example
  • Usage in context: The learner's sentence lined up with real sentences that share its key word, so the model's reading can be set against genuine uses.

Direct example: The Gyllenhaal Experiment

09 · Make · Openers

Mix to match

Blend two sources until the result matches a target.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Sets how much of each source goes into a blend.
What comes back
The blend updates against the target, with the mismatch shown.
As an opener
Set how much the spotlight trusts the camera and the microphone so it keeps up with the dancer.
As a summary
Weak fit. A blend slider changes a mechanism without naming a fix.
Watch for
If any blend near the middle works, the game has no decision. Make the right blend change with the case.
Keyboard and text route
The blend is a slider with its value beside it, and the mismatch is a number as well as a picture.
Pairs with, for example
  • Matched small multiples: Camera-only, microphone-only and blended tracks in matched panels on one time axis.

Our games: Crossfade (chooses which camera and microphone readings the spotlight combines)

10 · Make · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Reshape a fixed total

Move a fixed amount around and watch the chance of reaching a target change.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Redistributes a conserved quantity, such as prize money or probability, across outcomes.
What comes back
The shape changes while the total stays put, and the chance of reaching the target updates.
As an opener
A fixed amount of water fills twelve plant pots. Reshape the pours so as many plants as possible get enough.
As a summary
Probability is a fixed total. Show the old method's distribution, press the fix, and see where the mass moves while the total holds.
Watch for
Show the total holding at every move, and build the constraint into the control. A constraint enforced by greyed-out buttons reads as a bug.
Keyboard and text route
Each outcome has buttons that move one unit to or from it; the total and the chance of reaching the target are read as numbers.
Pairs with, for example
  • Counted unit tiles: The prize money as tiles moved between sectors, with the number of tiles fixed.
  • Observed against reference: The reshaped distribution drawn over the original as a dashed outline, so what moved shows against where it started.

Our games: Prize Wheel (reshapes the prizes under a fixed total to raise the chance of reaching the bar)

11 · Make · Openers

Tune a process

Change how a system evolves, then watch the path it takes.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Sets a few process settings, such as step size or randomness, and runs the process.
What comes back
The trajectory or sequence plays out and can be inspected afterward.
As an opener
Set how fast a thermostat reacts so the room reaches its target without overshooting or crawling.
As a summary
Weak fit. A process setting is a mechanism knob, and a summary slide needs one button named for a fix.
Watch for
Keep to about two settings that reshape the whole run, and prove by brute force that some setting reaches the goal on every round.
Keyboard and text route
Sliders with their values beside them; the run is summarized as text, such as where it stopped and how far it overshot.
Pairs with, for example
  • Matched small multiples: Runs under three settings in matched panels on the same axes, so the effect of a setting is a comparison rather than a memory.
  • Outcomes with an interval band: Many runs under one setting drawn as a band around their average path, so the spread is part of the result.

Our games: Downhill Racer (sets move strength and carry-over, then watches the run) · Next Note (sets the randomness and how many likely notes to keep, then hears the tunes)

12 · Arrange · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Sort into bins

Sort things by hand, then compare with how the method sorts them.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Drags items into bins.
What comes back
The method's sort appears beside the learner's, with the disagreements marked.
As an opener
Sort 12 photos into piles before the classifier does. The interesting moment is where you and it differ.
As a summary
Show the previous method's bins with misfiled items glowing. Press the fix and those items move.
Watch for
Agreeing with the method is the goal only when the method is right. Mark which sort the true labels support.
Keyboard and text route
Each item has a menu of bins, and each bin lists its contents.
Pairs with, for example
  • Relationship matrix: Rows for the learner's piles and columns for the classifier's; each off-diagonal cell opens the photos they disagree on.
  • Intersection plot: When a photo can sit in several piles, an intersection plot counts the photos in each combination.

Rank

Put a few cards in order, then see the method's order.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Orders a handful of cards.
What comes back
The method's order is revealed and the cards that moved are traced.
As an opener
Order five continuations of a sentence from likely to strange; the model's order follows.
As a summary
Hold the same five cards and reshuffle them under each method, so the change in order is the before and after.
Watch for
Keep it to five or six cards. A long list turns ranking into sorting work.
Keyboard and text route
Move up and move down buttons on each card; the order is read as a numbered list.
Pairs with, for example
  • Paired count strips: The learner's order and the model's as paired dots joined by lines, so the cards that moved stand out.

14 · Arrange · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Lay out a map

Place items so that neighbors belong together.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Drags items onto a board.
What comes back
The method's own layout of the same items appears beside the learner's.
As an opener
Arrange ten words on a table so neighbors make sense; the model's map then appears beside yours.
As a summary
Show the same words laid out before and after training.
Watch for
An authored map implies no measured distance. Say which map is measured.

Pair up

Match items one to one under a constraint.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Connects or swaps items into pairs.
What comes back
Each pairing is scored at once and the total updates.
As an opener
Match captions to photos with a limited number of swaps.
As a summary
Draw the matches as lines. Each method straightens more of them.

16 · Arrange · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Route and defer

Decide which cases the method handles alone and which go to limited human review.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Sets where the method's confidence hands a case to a person, within a review budget.
What comes back
Each case lands with the method or with a reviewer, and the results and the reviews left update.
As an opener
Set where a parcel-sorting machine hands parcels to its one human checker, so the checker's 20 looks go to the parcels the machine is least sure of.
As a summary
Open on the old method spending reviews on cases it already had right. Press the fix and see where the doubtful band falls under the new method, with the same review budget.
Watch for
The budget makes it a decision. Without one, sending everything to review always wins.
Keyboard and text route
Gates move with the arrow keys; each case's route and the reviews left are listed as text.
Pairs with, for example
  • Paired count strips: Each case's confidence as a dot on one scale, paired with its true outcome, with the review band drawn across.
  • Linked category lanes: Cases flowing into three stable lanes: kept, reviewed and discarded.

Our games: Photo Finish (sets two score gates that decide which shots the AI keeps, discards or sends to limited human review)

17 · Arrange · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Select channels and reconstruct

Choose what crosses a narrow link, then see what the far side rebuilds.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Picks which few channels, features or tracks a limited connection carries.
What comes back
The receiver rebuilds the original from what arrived, shown over the original.
As an opener
A link carries three numbers per photo. Choose which three let the far side redraw the face.
As a summary
Hold the budget fixed. Show the rebuild from the old method's channels, then press the fix and see the rebuild from the new method's channels.
Watch for
Compute the rebuild from the chosen channels. A drawn reconstruction teaches nothing about what was lost.
Keyboard and text route
Channels are toggled from a list, and the share of the original that the rebuild keeps is read as a number.
Pairs with, for example
  • Same-scene layers: The rebuild laid over the original, so what was lost shows as the uncovered part.
  • Matched small multiples: The rebuild from each choice of channels in matched panels beside the original.

Our games: Echo Dancer (chooses which two movement tracks the link carries)

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner

What comes back
The process shows its state at that moment.
As an opener
Rewind a denoising run to the step where the picture first appears.
As a summary

The learner
Zooms in or out.
What comes back
Each scale shows its own evidence: the overall pattern from far out, single records or boxes close in. Name on screen what each scale lets the learner check.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Picks one token, pixel or record and follows it.
What comes back
That item's state is shown at each stage while the rest fade.
As an opener

22 · Move through · Openers and summaries

Fork a run

Split one run at a moment, change one thing, and watch both branches.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Pauses a run, copies it, changes one condition in the copy, and plays both with the same randomness.
What comes back
The two branches run side by side from the fork, so any difference comes from the one change.
As an opener
A delivery drone drifts off course halfway through its flight. Pause it, change one setting in a copy, and run both from that moment to find the change that brings it home.
As a summary
At the moment the old method goes wrong, fork the run: one branch keeps the old method and the other applies the fix, with matched randomness. The learner chooses where to fork.
Watch for
Match the randomness in both branches and say so on screen, or a difference from noise reads as the effect of the change.
Keyboard and text route
The fork point moves with the arrow keys, and both branches are described step by step, including where they part.
Pairs with, for example
  • Matched small multiples: The two branches in synchronized panels from the fork onward, with an optional difference view.

25 · Probe and break · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Find the fault

Something is broken. Inspect the parts to find what.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Opens parts of a system to inspect them, with a limited number of looks.
What comes back
Each inspected part shows its input and its output.
As an opener
The pipeline gives a wrong answer. Find the faulty step in as few inspections as you can.
As a summary
Draw the failure large. The button names the assumption that broke, and the next method repairs it.

The fault must be findable from what the parts show, not from a lucky first pick. Vary which part is broken between rounds.

26 · Probe and break · Openers and summaries

Push until it breaks

Feed in harder and harder cases until it fails.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Moves a difficulty control or picks harder cases.
What comes back
The system visibly holds or fails at each step.
As an opener
How far can you push before it fails? Find the breaking point in as few tries as possible.
As a summary
Run the same ramp for each method and mark where each one fails, as computed from the methods themselves. A later method may hold longer, but not necessarily on every ramp.
Watch for
Failure along a ramp need not be steady: a method can fail, recover and fail again. Compute every outcome rather than drawing the expected story, and put the ramp in the learner's terms, such as more clutter or longer sentences.
Keyboard and text route
The difficulty is a slider with its step written beside it; holds and failures are listed.
Pairs with, for example
  • Paired count strips: For each difficulty step, paired dots for two methods, with failures marked where they occur.

27 · Probe and break · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Repair without regressions

Fix today's failure without breaking what already worked.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Changes a rule or method, then runs it against earlier cases before installing it.
What comes back
The current case and every earlier case pass or fail under the change.
As an opener
A spam filter has just let a scam through. Change one rule to catch it, then check the rule against the 20 messages the filter already handled.
As a summary
Give each slide a row of the earlier slides' cases. Press the fix and see the new case and the earlier ones together, so a fix that costs an old success shows.
Watch for
Keep the earlier cases visible and few. A long regression suite becomes a score to read rather than a pattern to see.
Keyboard and text route
Rules are chosen with buttons, and each case's pass or fail is listed with what changed.
Pairs with, for example
  • Relationship matrix: Rules as rows and earlier incidents as columns, each cell pass or fail, so a regression shows as one cell changing.
  • Event lanes with a state table: The failing incident's events in lanes beside the account's state, so the step the new rule changes is visible.

Our games: Transfer Desk (chooses a repeat rule, tests it against earlier incidents, then installs it)

28 · Probe and break · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Choose the next experiment

Two explanations fit everything so far. Find the input that tells them apart.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Builds an input on which two candidate rules predict different outcomes, then queries the hidden system.
What comes back
Each candidate's prediction appears, then the hidden system's answer settles which one survives.
As an opener
Two rules explain every example seen. Construct an input on which their predictions differ, then query the hidden system. Fewest queries wins.
As a summary
Show two methods that agree on every example so far, then the input on which they differ and what each does with it.
Watch for
Reward information, not luck. After a query both rules predicted alike, describe that the answer could not separate them.
Keyboard and text route
The input is built from labelled choices, and both predictions and the answer are listed as text.
Pairs with, for example
  • Relationship matrix: Candidate rules as rows and possible inputs as columns, each cell a prediction; the useful inputs are the columns where the rows disagree.

29 · Probe and break · Openers; summaries as a demonstration

Interleave events

Choose the order in which two processes take their steps, and find the order that breaks a rule.

Opener, summary and cautions
The learner
Advances one process or the other a step at a time, deciding the order of events.
What comes back
The shared state updates after each step, and a broken requirement is flagged at the step that broke it.
As an opener
Two cashiers update one shared tally. Choose who takes the next step until the tally comes out wrong, in as few steps as you can.
As a summary
Show the ordering that breaks the old method. Press the fix and replay the same ordering against the new one.
Watch for
Show only the steps that read or write the shared state. Vary which ordering breaks it between rounds, so replay changes the search.

Give the action a job in the unit.

The same action works differently in an opener and in a summary. Decide its role before designing the controls.

Unit opener

Pose the unit's question before its vocabulary. Write a brief: the scenario's facts, a cost, and the decision the player is scored on. Give about two levers at the level of the concept, and draw every setting that changes the best move in the scene, with its number beside it. Judge the single thing the player sees.

Unit summary

Hold one example across the unit's methods. Open each slide on the previous method's failure, drawn large, and offer one button named for the fix. The page does the work, and the slide ends on one line about what changed. A summary has no score and no check. The learner may still supply or mark the problem, since nothing judges that choice; a prediction whose answer is then revealed is a check, so it belongs in an opener.

After a miss in an opener

Describe the state on screen against the goal, with numbers, and never say which control to move. Replay should change the best move, not only the random seed. Count deliberate runs, not slider movements, and keep the game untimed.

A reason to press

In a summary, a control that only changes a number or a caption gives no reason to press it. Each button should be named for a fix and show a before and after. Mechanism knobs belong in a lecture widget with its own check.

Recipes for a learning question.

Each recipe pairs a visual method with an action. Like the pairings on the cards, they are starting points; the test is whether the learner can see why their action mattered.

Controlled forks

What did this one change cause?

Visual: Matched small multiples, synchronized panels, with an optional difference view. Action: Fork a run.

Matched randomness makes the difference between the panels the effect of the change, within this simulation.

Discriminating experiments

Which explanation is right?

Visual: Relationship matrix, rules as rows, tests as columns, predictions in the cells. Action: Choose the next experiment.

A column where the rows disagree is a test worth running, and its answer removes rows.

Execution traces

Why did the order of events break it?

Visual: Event lanes with a state table. Action: Interleave events, Repair without regressions.

The exact step that breaks the requirement is in view, with the state before and after it.

Residual inspection

Is the fit good where it matters?

Visual: a fitted view linked to signed residuals and held-out results (no card yet). Action: Draw freehand, Tune a process.

When a better fit on the observed points makes held-out predictions worse, the learner sees it in the residuals, not only in a score.

Coverage and uncertainty

How wide must a range be to be right often enough?

Visual: Outcomes with an interval band. Action: Set a range.

Wider ranges buy coverage at a visible cost.

Constraints and tradeoffs

What is the best choice under these limits?

Visual: a feasible region, or cost plotted against quality (no card yet). Action: Spend a budget, Reshape a fixed total.

Show the current choice during play. Reveal the full frontier afterward when showing it earlier would give the answer away.

Capacity and fit

Will it fit, and where does it not?

Visual: dimensioned shapes with clearance or coverage overlays (no card yet), as in Pockets. Action: placing an object under geometric constraints.

Feedback shows the uncovered or conflicting area itself, rather than only a success light.

Regrouping evidence

Does the pattern survive a different grouping?

Visual: Counted unit tiles. Action: Sort into bins.

Records keep their identity as they regroup, so an overall rate and the subgroup rates can be compared even when they point opposite ways.
