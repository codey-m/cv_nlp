How do a picture and a sentence meet?

1 · Fuse

Photo 1 is blurry. Its caption reads a dog on a beach. Is it a dog?

Trusting only the caption fails whenever the caption is wrong.

What changedAdditive fusion sums the two inputs. A gate scales each one by how clear it is.

2 · Coordinate

Now the caption a dog on a beach arrives alone. Find its photo among the five.

Fusing it with each photo in turn means one run per pair, and a photo library has millions.

What changedEach modality keeps its own encoder, joined only by a similarity score. That works only if the two spaces agree.

3 · Contrast

In the grid, photo 1 scores as high against an orange as against its own caption.

Bigger encoders trained separately still do not agree.

What changedEach batch pulls true pairs together and pushes the rest apart, so cosine now means “these match”. Write class names as captions, and the same grid is a classifier.

4 · Shape

Now swap in DINOv2 for the photos and MPNet for the captions, with no matched pairs.

CLIP needed hundreds of millions of pairs, and pairs are the expensive part.

What changedRelations agree across modalities even when coordinates do not, so the shapes can be lined up without pairs. Lab 5 tests how far that goes.

5 · Talk

Ask about photo 5: What color is the car?

Writing every possible answer as a caption does not scale.

What changedA small adapter turns the image into tokens the language model reads, so it writes text conditioned on the image.
