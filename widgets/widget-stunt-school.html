    The idea behind the game

    Pip makes two choices: an aim, then a strength. At first every option is equally likely. Practice scores are compared with a running expectation. A better-than-expected score makes the choices in that attempt more likely; a worse one makes them less likely. This is a small policy-gradient learner.

    The encouragement rule changes the training reward. The show's goal stays fixed: bounce the ball through the hoop. A learner can therefore earn plenty of practice points while practising something that does not complete the trick. Experimentation adds random choices and keeps the preferences from becoming too fixed. Pip carries those preferences to each new hoop.

    The ball physics and creature are an authored simulation. The throws, scores and learning updates are computed. Practice stays in this page and is not saved after you close it.
