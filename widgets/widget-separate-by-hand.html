    How to play

    The goal is a play where every act comes out the way its sketch shows, with as few linked pairs as you can. Par is the fewest that work.

    Write the acts after Act 1. Each starts as a copy of Act 1's words. Drag a word onto another to swap them, or press one word and then the other. Arrow keys move a focused word.

    Rehearse to watch the actors perform the play, act by act. Each act gets a tick or a cross.

    The director stages whatever it reads in each line. After the first rehearsal, the row under each line shows what it reads.

    Link two neighbouring words by pressing the gap between them. The director then also reads that pair, in every act where it appears. Press the gap again to unlink.

    The idea behind the game

    Counting words loses who did what

    The director reads a line as a bag of words: which words appear and how many times, with the order thrown away. Every act in a play uses the same words, so every act makes the same bag. Anything the director learns for one act it must also stage for the others, so no choice of word points can tell them apart.

    A linked pair is a bigram: two neighbouring words read as one feature. A pair only helps if it appears in some acts and not others. When the tables turn, “dog chases” becomes “robot chases”, while “happy dog” stays in both acts. When only the moods swap, it is the other way round: “dog chases” is in both acts and “happy dog” is in one. The useful pair is whichever one differs.

    Negation is a different problem. “Is happy”, “is not happy”, “is sad” and “is not sad” all read differently, but the word not has to push toward sad after happy and toward happy after sad. One point per word cannot do both, so rehearsal never fits all four. A pair such as “not happy” gives the director a feature of its own for the flipped meaning.

    The director is a linear classifier over these features, like the one in the lecture: for each decision it adds up a weight for every word and linked pair the line contains, and stages the option with the highest total. Lines with the same features get the same totals, whatever weights it learns.

    Reading every pair of neighbouring words, or longer runs of words (n-grams), is the classic fix. It keeps some local order, and the number of features grows quickly.

    The director is a small learner written for this game. During rehearsal it learns points for each word and linked pair it can read, one set per decision (who acts, what they do, or the mood), and stops when every line comes out right or after 400 passes. No language model runs. Lines are built from four characters, four actions and two moods. Par is the fewest links that make every scene right, found by checking every combination of pairs in the finished lines.
